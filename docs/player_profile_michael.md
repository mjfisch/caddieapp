## Snapshot

- **Index:** 2.1 (eff. 05/14/2026). All-time low 0.4. Career scoring avg 76 over 94 rounds.
- **Goal:** return to low/mid-70s consistently and control the game. **Not** chasing scratch.
- **Ball flight:** driver = fade (L→R). Irons split by length: **long clubs (3h, 4i, 6i) fade/push, the 5h draws, and 7i down through the wedges run straight-to-baby-draw.** The blanket "irons draw" is too broad — see dossier.
- **Signature constraint:** persistent **two-way driver miss**, but it is NOT symmetric — the feared and frequent miss is the **pull / snap-hook LEFT**, not the fade right. **Treat left as the real danger side off the tee.** Structural, manage strategically; do not coach it out mid-round.
- **Defining leak:** the **press-and-compound** pattern — but now precisely localized (below). It lives on **tee shots and putting only**; iron/approach decision-making is sound and survives pressure.
- **The detonator chain (most important single insight):** chronic short lag speed → first putt finishes outside 4 ft → 3-putt → 3-putt is his #1 emotional trigger → presses on the next tee → double. The putting leak is the *ignition* for the tee leak. They are one problem, not two.
- **Temperament:** pressure-neutral. Confident under pressure; nerves are not a source of bad shots; no tighten-up moments (first tee, breaking a number, closing stretch). Risk is one-directional — he **presses, never freezes.**
- **Relative strengths:** par-5 scoring (career 5.1), iron/approach play and decision-making, scrambling relative to fairways hit, strong starts, green reading, short game shot selection.
- **Relative weaknesses:** lag putting speed (chronic short), double-bogey clustering driven by two specific aggression leaks (driveable par 4, hero recovery), fairway accuracy (career ~41–50%, treated as a fixed feature, not a fault).

## Strategic defaults (IF / THEN rules for the caddie)

### Top objective
- **Double-bogey avoidance is the #1 strategic objective.** Target 0–1 doubles/round. Doubles come from the *second* decision after a mistake, not the first mistake — guard the compound.
- **Par 4s are the scoring engine** (target ~4.1–4.2). **GIR target 10–12/round; scrambling floor 40%.**

### Tee shots
- **Default: driver, "get it as close as possible."** He is a 2.1 and hits a usable in-play driver more often than not — do not under-club him into 200+ yd approaches to chase safety.
- **The Grunt Fade is the stock pressure tee shot:** tee slightly lower, slight choke-up, set up for the fade, *hold the face open, ~100% effort.* Caddie can call it by name.
- **HARD RULE (bag-wide): never guide, never throttle down, never "swing easy to find the fairway."** Reduced speed / steering produces his worst misses (esp. the snap hook). Commit fully or pick a club he can commit to fully. This applies to every full-swing club.
  - *Important nuance:* the no-throttle rule is about **swing SPEED**, not about partial shots. He freely and comfortably manufactures distance via **swing length, added/subtracted loft, and ball position** (three-quarters, knockdowns). Caddie may call for those. Just never "ease up" on it.
- **IF trouble LEFT → flag it as the real danger side.** Call the Grunt Fade, cue full commitment + face held open + no easing up. The feared outcome is specifically the **snap hook**; a straight pull he can usually live with.
- **IF trouble RIGHT → this is the comfortable hole.** Same Grunt Fade, aim adjusted farther left. The fade rarely leaks far right.
- **3h-for-position (underused — caddie should proactively offer):** on holes where a 3h leaves a full wedge / short-iron approach, surface the 3h play. BUT name the 3h's right-miss so he commits to it rather than duffing it. He believes (feel, not data) the 3h-for-position has worked and wants to do it more.
- **Bail-out logic is PERFORMANCE-based, not nerves-based.** Do NOT offer a bail club to "settle him" — nerves aren't his issue and that framing will annoy him. Offer the 3h **only when (a) driver has already produced double-causing misses *this round* AND (b) the hole is short enough that the 3h still leaves a playable approach.** If he's scoring badly but driver isn't the cause, stay with the Grunt Fade.
- **Driver max pattern (4" tee):** green-light the longer/max driver only when the hole is **wide open AND downwind.** Otherwise stock — the max set scatters badly (offline SD ~34 yds vs ~14 stock).

### Approach play
- **Default: aim to the fat side of the pin (~15–20 ft), short-side consequence priced in pre-shot.** This is genuine, present discipline — and it **survives pressure** (his tilt does not bleed into approaches). Caddie can largely trust his iron decisions even in a sliding round.
- **Green-light flags when there's no bad miss either side** (e.g., middle pin, open green, irons feeling good) — that's correct aggression, not a leak.
- **Front pin over water:** club to a number he reaches **without overswinging** (favor the longer club when between). Aggression scales **inversely with club length** — green-light wedge attacks, dial back longer-iron ones.
- **Back pin / trouble long:** default to the **low running approach** — land front-to-middle, release. Accepted miss = checks early, leaves a long putt (he's at peace with it). Never the high carry-to-the-flag gamble. He owns this shot; caddie may call it explicitly (esp. 9i knockdown, 125–145).
- **In-between clubs:** caddie may freely call three-quarter/knockdown shots. His miss here is a **wind/firmness misjudgment**, not a swing failure — so when between clubs, the caddie's value is sharpening the **condition read**, not picking the swing. Neither up-smooth nor down-hard is systematically worse for him.
- **Long-club hierarchy (183–195 zone): 5h > 4i, always.** Aim for an easy two-putt target on long par 3s; never a hero line into a tight green.

### Par 5s (exploit — relative strength)
- **Go-for-it trigger (either condition green-lights):** (a) a yardage he's confident reaches the green, OR (b) the likely *miss* still leaves a good up-and-down-for-birdie (not short-sided, not a long bunker shot). **Reachable + good drive → he always goes, never lays up to a number.** This aggression is *earned* — let him have it.
- **Layup:** his instinct is "as close as possible without compromising the up-and-down" (does NOT dial to a set number). **Caddie option to surface (not default):** "closest-possible vs. full-wedge number" — offer laying to his **favorite 105 (full SW)** when realistic, and let the engine numbers make the case. Partial/awkward wedges (40–70 yd) are lower-percentage for most players; a comfortable full number may convert better.

### Trouble / recovery — THE double-bogey zone
- **Tendency:** in trees/trouble with a tempting gap and a reachable green, he defaults to the **hero shot.** Same dopamine as the driveable par 4. He can pull it off *more than expected*, **but it is NOT the majority outcome and more often than not makes a double.** "Nothing to lose, I'm ten over" removes all restraint.
- **GUARDRAIL (firmest in the bag, by his explicit instruction): the caddie STATES the chip-out / advance-to-a-number play as THE CALL — not as one option among two.** It names the pattern once: *"This is the hero-shot spot — it makes a double more often than it works. We advance to [number], we still make par or worst-case bogey."* Then the play.
  - **Override the "ten-over, nothing to lose" logic explicitly:** every avoided double is a stroke that counts; a bad round is a reason to stop the bleeding, not a license to gamble.
- **Fairway bunker (~180):** confirmed **no-press zone.** He accepts being short — takes **6i/7i**, contact-first. **Won't hit hybrid from sand; won't trust the 4i there.** Caddie does NOT offer the green-reachable long club from sand.
- **Contact-pressure lie rule (sand / tight / awkward): 4i and hybrids come OUT of the bag** → fall back to clean-contact mid-irons (6i/7i). Hybrids are a tee/fairway tool, not a recovery tool.

### Around the green
- **Principle: the LIE dictates aggression, not the pin.**
- **Short-sided, cushioned lie (rough):** semi-flop / high pitch is a **trusted, owned shot** — green-light it.
- **Short-sided, bare/tight lie:** back-foot, **contact-first**, manage loft + backswing length to **leave a putt.** Never a flop off a bare lie (his own rule). Discipline fully intact here.
- **Fringe:** decided by length + whether crossing tiers. Default lean **putt** ("a bad putt beats a bad chip"); comfortable chipping when needed.
- **Bunker:** confidence keyed to **sand firmness first**, then distance, then room to roll out. Caddie surfaces firmness + available green, not just yardage.

### Putting
- **Lag (40+):** intent is correct — trying to get **tap-in close, not make it.** Leak is **speed: chronic SHORT miss, often well short. No directional bias** (strong green reader).
  - **Caddie cue (no-practice-friendly, deliverable cold): give a target PAST the hole** — "lag it to ~2 ft *past*, not to the cup." Reframes the speed task so his natural short bias finishes hole-high instead of 4 ft short. A *thought*, not a drill.
- **Short putts (≤4 ft):** strength. Confident, **not a 3-putt source.** Rare miss = **over-read (playing break that isn't there) or too firm through the break** — an *assertive* error, not tentative. If anything, watch the **over-read**.
- **3-putt root cause = first-putt distance control.** Fix the lag speed and the 3-putts (and the detonator chain) go with it.
- **Reads:** strong green reader. Caddie supports **speed/distance**, stays out of the **line** on lags.

## Pressure / tilt profile

- **Tilt is club-dependent and localized: it fires on TEE SHOTS and PUTTING only.** It does **not** reach his iron/approach play even in a sliding round. Concentrate interventions there; trust his irons.
- **The tilt WINDOW is a sequence, not a number:** a bad front (e.g. +5) he absorbs — **the turn is a genuine mental reset** ("fresh 9, shoot even, respectable"). The danger is a **bad front FOLLOWED BY an early-back-nine stumble** (≈ +2 through holes 10–12). That combination is the trigger.
  - **Do NOT intervene on the front nine.** A rough front alone is not a tilt signal — over-managing it would be wrong. Intervention point = early back struggle stacked on a bad front.
- **Trigger hierarchy (most→least damaging):**
  1. **A 3-putt** — top trigger, *because it devalues the good ball-striking that got him there.* Primary ignition for the tee press.
  2. **Cold / rain weather** (wind less so). A standing **tilt-risk multiplier** — a miserable day starts him closer to the edge before any bad hole; lower the hold-the-line threshold on cold/wet days.
  3. A flubbed short up-and-down.
  4. A **snap-hook** drive (a straight pull is tolerable).
  5. Slow play — minor.
  6. Playing partners — negligible.
- **Direction under pressure:** presses / more aggressive. Never tentative.

### What the caddie does about it
- **Direct call-out, by name.** No quiet-steering, no backing off. He can take it and prefers it (all rounds competitive-but-not-Tour-serious; honest > cushioned). Register: *"This is the spot — bad front, rough start to the back, you just three-putt. This is where you press and make a double. We're not doing it. Here's the play."*
- **Calibrated, NOT constant.** Fires only at the mapped combination (bad front + early-back stumble / a 3-putt detonator / a cold-wet day). Not on the front, not on isolated bad holes, not for partners or pace. Frequency is what keeps it credible — spread everywhere it becomes wallpaper.
- **No casual/competitive split** — single register: honest, direct, unbothered.

### Compliance model (state-dependent) — important
- **When CALM (default, most shots):** call + brief *why*. He's a thinking player who engages with the reasoning.
- **When TILTING (the call-out moment):** **INSTRUCTION ONLY.** Name the pattern once, then the bare play — *"Chip out. Wedge to 105. Move on."* No 80-vs-84 logic, no "every double counts." Once he's hot, reasoning reads as patronizing and slows compliance.
- **Tilt overrides verbosity globally:** any guardrail that normally carries a reason (e.g. the recovery rule) collapses to instruction-only once the press is firing.

### Pre-committed framework (the guardrails, collected)
1. **After any trouble/penalty shot:** the next shot is the one that **guarantees back-in-play at a number he likes. No second gamble. One mistake doesn't get a chaser.** (His established hard rule.)
2. **Recovery (trees/gap):** caddie states the chip-out as the call, not an option (above).
3. **After a 3-putt:** heightened alert on the **next tee** (the detonator), not just the next green — direct call-out if the tilt window is also open.
4. **Never** prescribe an easy/guided swing to find a fairway.
5. **Cold/wet:** lower the intervention threshold preemptively.

### Cross-club patterns the caddie should know
- **Directional miss rotates with club length:** long clubs (5h, 6i, also 3h/4i) **block/push RIGHT**; scoring clubs (7i, 8i, GW, SW) **overdraw LEFT**; wedges around the green miss on **contact** (fat/toe), not direction.
- **Trust spine = scoring clubs.** PW, 9i, GW, SW all trust 5. Everything **6-iron and longer is the "needs a reason" end**; 4i is the genuine low-trust outlier (2).
- **5h is the trusted long club** and the lone right-to-left long option.
- **Anchor club:** PW. **Hate-but-keep:** 4i. **Problem long club:** 4i (scatter + path/face right-miss).

### Data caveats (apply bag-wide)
- **All measured carries are MAT / RANGE-BALL / COLD-SPRING numbers.** On grass, premium ball, warm summer they likely run a touch longer. **Treat the workbook carries as a cold-weather floor**, not the warm-round number. Lean to the shorter club / "this plays a touch longer today" until re-captured on grass in warm weather. (Most visible: SW data says 96, he plays it to ~105.)
- **Dispersion "score" is an ordinal consistency rating (1–9, lower = tighter), not lateral yards** — rank-orders clubs, can't be drawn as an ellipse. True lateral dispersion needs the shot-by-shot side values computed to SD; until then lateral spread is seeded from the benchmark table even for clubs with measured carry.
- **Driver: leave PENDING behind → partial.** Real measured shots exist but small/old/indoor sample. Re-map on a Trackman tee bay to put it on the same footing as the rest of the bag (highest-value remaining data task).

## Course & conditions

*Interviewed 2026-06-02. These are cross-format reads; they inform both tracks.*

### Wind
- **Knockdown is a trusted, owned shot — not a survival reflex.**
- **Into-wind + crosswind:** default to the **knockdown**, and he actively reads how the wind affects **both flight and landing/release** (not just "club up"). Crosswind: works it, comfortable either holding or riding.
- **Downwind:** normal swing, **expects extra carry** scaled to distance and how high he expects to launch it (high ball rides more).
- **Net:** wind is a **managed variable, not a disproportionate weakness** — the flighted shot is his fallback, so he isn't exposed the way a one-trajectory player is. The caddie's value in wind is **sharpening the condition read, not picking the swing** (ties to the locked between-club rule: his miss there is a wind/firmness misjudgment, not a swing failure).

### Cold / wet / early
- **Carry drops ~one club at ~50°F vs. the ~75° baseline.** Anchor it correctly to avoid double-counting: the workbook carries are the **cold-mat floor**, so on a cold day the book number is about right (maybe a hair under); on a **warm** day add ~a club's worth of expectation. Same one-club spread, just anchored at the cold end.
- **Layers restrict the swing (mechanical, the important one):** bulky cold/wet gear makes a free turn harder. This is the *physical* reason the **cold/wet tilt multiplier** (locked, pressure section) is real — a raw day starts him with a compromised turn, so misses come easier and frustration builds faster.
- **Cold-day caddie package:** club up one · expect a restricted turn · **hold the no-throttle rule HARDER** (a steered swing on top of a restricted one is exactly the snap-hook recipe) · lower the tilt-intervention threshold preemptively.

### Walking vs. cart
- **Cart always. Never assume walking.** No walking-fatigue variable to model; do not frame advice around "save your legs."

### Late-round focus (mild — footnote, not a pillar)
- A **mild, occasional, across-the-board focus drift** late in rounds. Not every round, low impact — explicitly **not** a structural feature and **distinct from tilt** (drift = attention erosion, not frustration).
- **Caddie behavior:** a light **re-engagement nudge** on closing holes *when relevant* — quiet "commit to this one / name the shot," never confrontational. **Do NOT** treat a loose closing-hole shot as a tilt trigger or fire the by-name call-out — wrong tool. Drift gets the nudge; the 10–12 emotional spike gets the direct call. Keep them from bleeding together.

### Firm vs. soft — a genuine seasonal edge
- **Strong preference for FIRM-AND-FAST; it's an edge, not just a comfort.** Low trajectory, bump-and-runs around the green, happy to **run mid/long irons onto accessible greens.** A ground-game player, and firm rewards exactly that.
- **Confirmed by his own scoring:** June–August clearly beats April/May/September.
- **This is the WHY behind the locked back-pin default** (low running approach, land front, release) — that shot *needs* firm ground, so it's a summer weapon that quietly loses value when soft.
- **Shoulder-season double penalty:** Apr/May/Sep are both **colder** (a club shorter, restricted turn in layers) **and softer** (ground game stripped) — a cleaner explanation for the seasonal scoring gap than weather alone, and the same window the cold/wet tilt multiplier is live.
- **Caddie adjustment (symmetric):** firm summer → lean into the ground game, run it up freely, get out of his way. Soft spring/fall → expect approaches to stop, dial back the run-up, accept the A-game setup isn't there, and work hardest on tilt management.

## Format: Scramble (Track 2)

*Different game — do NOT inherit stroke-play conservatism, and do NOT inherit its caution either. Interviewed 2026-06-02. The standing crew: Michael (2.1), **Matt (0.0, 300+ bomber, hits LAST)**, Jacob (8), Nick (10). Correction to old pre-seed: this is NOT a blind "driver every hole, accept the two-way miss" role — see below.*

### Team structure & order
- **Tee net is THREE deep behind Michael's lead-off ball.** Jacob and Nick are real tee contributors — they carry it as far as Michael or slightly more, not passengers. Matt is the closer who hits last.
- **Role (agreed by the team):** Michael goes **first off the tee on par 4s and 5s** (no ball down yet), and **third on approaches / chips / putts** (two balls already in), scaling aggression to how the first two landed.

### Tee shots (lead-off)
- **Default driver — and MORE often than on his own card**, because the deep net means a wild one isn't fatal. Do not under-club him for safety here; let it eat.
- **The 3h comes OUT LESS than in stroke play** (the inverse of his stroke-play "use it more" instinct). Reserve it for **genuinely demanding holes** — tight, real trouble, **especially LEFT** (the snap-hook side). Performance-based, never nerves; same logic as stroke play, just with a deeper cushion.

### Scoring shots (third in order) — the three-state aggression dial
The caddie **must know who is still to hit behind Michael** to run this. Aggression is a function of team state, not personality:
1. **A good ball is already down** (e.g. a teammate inside ~15 ft, or in the fairway with a wedge) → **house money. Fire at the flag, target inside 5 ft**, take the tuck on. Putts: more aggressive — less break, firmer — **but with a hard pace cap** (never so firm that speed shrinks the effective hole; his stroke-play short-putt discipline still governs pace). The Ringer green-lights the *line*, not the pace.
2. **Genuinely first, nothing down yet** → **moderate.** Driver eats; secure a usable, non-throwaway ball without playing scared.
3. **First two came up empty / unusable** → **MORE conservative than his own stroke play, on purpose.** Secure a usable ball so the player behind him (Matt) is freed to attack. This is a *setup*, not caution — the state most teams play backwards.

### The guardrail flip (the headline of the scramble track)
- His two firmest stroke-play lockdowns **flip GREEN when a ball is safe:**
  - **Driveable par 4** → green-light. Send it.
  - **Hero recovery from trouble** → green-light. Take the gap on.
- Same shots the caddie shuts down as *the call* on his own card become **weapons** in a scramble. The downside that makes them doubles in stroke play is a free roll with a ball already down. **Never inherit the stroke-play recovery/par-4 guardrails into a safe-ball scramble situation.**

### Pressure (scramble-specific)
- **Mildly elevated vs. his own ball, but it is CONTRIBUTION pressure, not status.** He wants to *produce the stroke-gainer* — stuff one inside 5, drain a 20-footer, drive a par 4, reach a par 5 in two, chip in. **Not** weakest-link anxiety, and **no agenda** about it being *his* ball that gets used — team success is the whole motive. Pressure-neutral temperament holds; this sharpens execution rather than tightening him up.
- Default scramble persona: **The Ringer** (team-aware). Expose the team-state toggle (ball safe / first to hit) so the dial above runs each shot.

## Coaching preferences

- **Default persona: Rain Man.** Flat, factual, numbers-first, no hype/hand-holding — he doesn't need it and finds it noise. Pairs with "call + brief why when calm" below.
- **Press auto-suggest: Beta, OFFER-and-wait.** When the press fires (the 10–12 emotional spike or a 3-putt detonator), the app **offers** a switch to Beta for a few holes — he accepts or waves it off. He stays in control of the bag even mid-tilt (matches the tone library's "offer, never force"). **Important split:** the **by-name call-out still fires automatically** at the trigger; only the **voice change is opt-in**. Sequence: trigger → instruction-only call-out in Rain Man's voice → *and* surface the Beta offer.
- **Detail over the ball:** call + brief **why when calm**; **instruction only when tilting.** (Locked core.)
- **Tell me when I'm wrong?** Yes — directly, by name, at the calibrated trigger moments. He takes a direct call-out and prefers it.
- **After a good shot: SILENCE — just move to the next.** No acknowledgment, no praise (consistent with Rain Man's outcome-neutral register and his pressure-neutral temperament). This gags *praise*, not information — a genuinely needed call on the **next** shot still comes. Even-keel both directions: bad shots get no drama either.
- **Never say / do:**
  1. **No swing mechanics over the ball** (his one hard rule). No mechanical cues during a shot — he's self-coached and a swing thought degrades commitment, which is what produces his steered miss. **Boundary:** naming a *shot* (Grunt Fade, knockdown, low runner, bump-and-run) is shot **selection**, not a swing thought — those calls are welcome. Mechanics ("flatten the wrist," "slow the transition") = off-limits.
  2. **No manufactured big-moment pressure.** "This one's for 74, bear down" is wrong — he's pressure-neutral; that framing invents hesitation that isn't there.
- **Past-shot references & blunt honesty:** fine. Doesn't bother him. Honest over flattering stands.
